What is this?

01 — The idea

Most apps want your attention.
This one wants your curiosity back.

What it is

One place for the student part of your life: a calm study room with a focus timer and tasks, your timetable and deadlines, group chats for friends, study circles, classes and events, a private file archive, and a small daily corner for rest.

Who it's for

People who fall into a Wikipedia hole about neutron stars at 1 a.m. People who draw in margins, write fiction nobody has read yet, build things, collect obscure music. Students who want their tools to feel as interesting as the things they're curious about.

Why it exists

Student apps are spreadsheets with a mascot. Chat apps were built for everyone, so they're built for nobody's studying. And none of them show you anything back about yourself except a streak to feel guilty about. We wanted something stranger, and kinder.

02 — Inside

A world that is made of
what you actually do.

Every account gets a world. It isn't decoration — each feature is a number from your real life, and the World page shows you which. Try it:

  1. Now

    Your day, as an orbit

    The home screen is one instrument: a 24-hour ring with your classes, study blocks and deadlines on it, your world at the centre, and a short log of what needs you right now.

  2. Study Room

    Deep space at midnight

    Almost nothing on screen. A focus timer that survives reloads, today's tasks, generated ambient sound, and a quiet history of the sessions you finished.

  3. Signals

    Conversations sorted by what they're for

    People, crews, study circles, classes, projects — and event rooms that open with a code, have a host at the door, and end on schedule. Messages are encrypted on your device.

  4. Archive

    Files as catalogued artifacts

    Upload, preview and download notes, papers, slides and recordings. Private unless you share them with a circle, and labelled so you always know what something is and where it came from.

  5. World

    A record you can see

    Land from focus, lights from finished tasks, aurora from creative work, moons for your goals. Discoveries mark the milestones. Time away shows as weather — never as loss.

  6. Drift

    Recover, discover, return

    Three things a day — something true, something to make, something to play — and then the door closes. No feed. Nothing to scroll.

03 — Is this for you?

Probably, if you…

  • have more tabs open than finished assignments, and the tabs are about black holes
  • want a group chat for a hackathon that doesn't need forty strangers' phone numbers
  • study better when the room is quiet and the clock is honest
  • keep notes, sketches and recordings you'd like to actually find again
  • like interfaces that are a little strange, as long as they make sense

Probably not, if you want another feed. There isn't one, on purpose.

04 — Safety and privacy

Built for students,
so built to be careful.

No phone numbers

You're a username. Nobody can browse the list of people here — they need your exact name to find you.

Rooms with a door

Event rooms open with a code, and a host lets each person in. Rooms can be set so only hosts post, and they end when the event does.

Encrypted on your device

Messages and chat files are encrypted before they leave your browser; the server stores ciphertext. Your key is protected by your password. Exactly what that does and doesn't cover.

Block and report

Blocking someone hides their messages from you and stops them adding you to chats. Reports go to the people who run this Panalo.

Files stay yours

The archive is private by default. Anything you share with a circle is readable by that circle and nobody else.

No ads, no AI, no feed

Nothing you write is sold, shown to advertisers, or sent to an AI service. There's no algorithm deciding what you see.
